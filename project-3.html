Capacity of Bike Racks Across Texas A&M University Campus Details:

Spring 2025

Problem

The bike racks at Texas A&M University have seen areas in where the bike racks were overcrowded. The key investigation was to search for the locations that were the most crowded and the least crowded by using ArcGIS Field Maps for real-world locations.

My Role

Recorded bike rack data from North Campus of Texas A&M University at day & night using the ArcGIS Field Maps App with accuracy coordinates using real GPS coordinates plus wrote the Methods portion of the project poster explaining the process of data collection and data sharing.

Data & Method

Investigated bike racks across different parts of the Texas A&M campus to find bike racks that were overcrowded and least crowded using combined group data across all parts of the Texas A&M University campuses including the North, Central, West, and the apartments.

Using the combined data recorded through ArcGIS Field Maps, we created a map to include all the bike rack locations as well as the capacity of the bike racks from day & night. Here's the recorded points below:

Map

What I Found

Data found that many bike racks that were full in the nighttime were areas which students lived as well as the students may travel on bikes during the day which explains the emptier bike racks in the daytime. Bike racks across Raymond Stotzer Parkway located north of campus and University Drive were mostly empty which we inferred that many students found different types of transportation like cars and buses to get over to these parts of campus.

What I Would Do Differently

Although we recorded the locations at day & night, the results may differ from every other day due to our scheduling conflicts with classes.

For more accuracy with the data, I would want to try and get a week's results of the bike racks to improve the quality of the data.
